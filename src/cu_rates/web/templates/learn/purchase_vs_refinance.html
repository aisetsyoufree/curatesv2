Purchase vs. Refinance: Why Refinance Is a Purpose, Not a Product

A Refinance Replaces an Existing Mortgage

A mortgage can be used to buy a home. A refinance is different: it pays off an existing mortgage and creates a new one. A refinance is an example of a mortgage that is not used to buy the home.

Reasons to Refinance

People refinance for different reasons. The new loan can:

  • Change the rate or the term: The new mortgage can seek a lower interest rate, a longer or shorter term, or a switch from an adjustable rate to a fixed rate.
  • Take cash out: Home equity is the difference between the value of the home and the balance owed. A cash-out refinancing borrows more than the amount owed and pays the difference in cash.

A lender considering a refinance looks at income, assets, credit, other debts, the value of the home, and the amount requested. If the loan amount compared with the home's value falls outside the lender's guidelines, the lender may decline the loan or offer less favorable terms than the existing mortgage.

Purchase and Refinance Are Different Transactions

A purchase loan buys the home. A refinance replaces a mortgage the owner already has. A cash-out refinance also pays the borrower cash above the amount owed. A quoted mortgage rate does not, by itself, say which of those transactions it is for.
