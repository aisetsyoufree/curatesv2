APR vs. Note Rate: Why They Are Never Interchangeable

Two Distinct Financial Measurements

The mortgage interest rate and the annual percentage rate (APR) measure different things. The interest rate is the yearly cost of borrowing the money. It does not reflect fees or other charges. The APR is a broader measure: it reflects the interest rate, any points, mortgage broker fees, and other charges paid to get the loan. The APR is usually higher than the interest rate.

The Interest Rate

  • The interest rate is the cost of borrowing, expressed as a yearly percentage. It does not include fees or other charges.
  • On a fixed-rate mortgage, the interest rate is set when the loan is made and does not change.
  • On an adjustable-rate mortgage, the rate may change after an introductory period. The rate is an index plus a set margin, subject to any caps. The APR on an adjustable-rate loan does not reflect the maximum rate the loan can reach.

The Annual Percentage Rate (APR)

The APR reflects the interest rate plus points, mortgage broker fees, and other charges. Because of those charges, the APR is usually higher than the interest rate. The APR of a closed-end loan includes fees. The APR of a home equity line of credit does not, so the two figures do not count fees the same way.

Comparison Rules and Limitations

Compare the interest rate with the interest rate, and read the APR as the broader cost figure. A lower interest rate can still come with points and fees that the APR includes. Do not compare the APR of a fixed-rate loan with the APR of an adjustable-rate loan, or the APRs of different adjustable-rate loans, as if those APRs showed the highest rate the adjustable loan can reach.
