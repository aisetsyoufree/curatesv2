The Limits of Shopping and Comparing Advertised Rates

An Advertised Rate Is Not the Whole Cost

The interest rate alone does not describe the cost of a mortgage. The annual percentage rate includes the interest rate plus other credit costs, such as points and mortgage insurance, which makes offers easier to compare. A lower rate that comes with extra points is not automatically a lower-cost loan.

An advertisement that calls a rate low or fixed may be describing a rate that lasts only for a short introductory period. Borrowers whose credit histories have more problems may be offered a higher APR than borrowers with fewer problems.

An Advertisement Is Not a Loan Offer

A rate in an advertisement is not a commitment to lend. For covered loans, federal law requires the lender to provide a Loan Estimate within three business days of receiving the application. That coverage is for closed-end consumer mortgages the Loan Estimate rule applies to, and it does not include every credit product. That form states the estimated interest rate, the monthly payment, and the closing costs, including estimates for taxes and insurance.

Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure

The Know Before You Owe mortgage disclosure rule replaced four older disclosure forms with two forms, the Loan Estimate and the Closing Disclosure. The Loan Estimate is meant to make it easier to compare offers from more than one lender. The consumer also gets three business days to review the Closing Disclosure before closing.

Informational Tables Versus Official Disclosures

Public comparison tables are general educational guides. They are not a Loan Estimate or a Closing Disclosure, and they are not a commitment to lend at the rate shown.
