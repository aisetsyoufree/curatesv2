Loan Eligibility: Why an Advertised Rate May Not Apply to You

What Changes an Offered Rate

The rate a lender offers can change with the credit score, the down payment, the loan term, and the loan type. A higher credit score generally means a lower interest rate and more lenders to choose from. A larger down payment typically lowers the interest rate and reduces the amount borrowed. A shorter loan term generally means a lower interest rate. Loans from VA, USDA, or FHA can offer lower interest rates for borrowers who qualify.

Published examples of those differences, including the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's rate examples, assume a single-family house used as a primary residence unless the example says otherwise. Those examples do not describe a price for a different occupancy or a different property type.

Down Payment, Mortgage Insurance, and Debt-to-Income

  • Private Mortgage Insurance: On a conventional loan, a down payment of less than twenty percent of the purchase price can mean the lender requires private mortgage insurance. That insurance protects the lender if payments stop. The same requirement usually applies when a conventional loan is refinanced with equity of less than twenty percent of the home's value.
  • Debt-to-Income Ratio: Debt-to-income ratio is monthly debt payments divided by gross monthly income. Lenders use it as one way to measure the ability to repay. Different loan products and lenders use different debt-to-income limits.
