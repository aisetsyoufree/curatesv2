Mortgage Points and Upfront Financing Costs

What Are Mortgage Points?

Points, also called discount points, lower the interest rate in exchange for paying more at closing. One point equals one percent of the loan amount. Points are paid at closing and are part of the closing costs.

Some lenders use the word "points" for any upfront fee calculated as a percentage of the loan amount, whether or not the interest rate is lower. Points listed on the Loan Estimate and the Closing Disclosure must be connected to a discounted interest rate.

Points and Other Upfront Fees

  • Points and the rate: Points are fees paid to the lender or broker and are often linked to the interest rate. Usually, paying more points means a lower rate. At the same lender, for the same kind of loan, a loan with one point should have a lower interest rate than a loan with zero points.
  • Other upfront fees: A home loan can also include other fees, such as a loan origination or underwriting fee. Those fees are separate from points. Points are the fees that are often linked to the interest rate.

Paying More Now or Paying More Later

Paying points means a higher cost at closing and a lower interest rate, so less is paid over time. That tradeoff depends on how long the loan is kept. Lender credits work in the other direction: a higher interest rate in exchange for money toward closing costs.

Why Points Matter in Public Comparisons

A rate that includes points is not the same offer as a zero-point rate. Comparisons need both the interest rate and the points, because the APR also includes points, broker fees, and certain other charges.
