Mortgage Basics and Core Principles

What Is a Mortgage?

A residential mortgage is a legal agreement in which a borrower receives financing from a lender to purchase or refinance real property, pledging the real estate itself as collateral for the debt obligation.

If the borrower fulfills the payment terms stipulated in the promissory note, the lender releases the lien upon full repayment. If the borrower defaults on the contractual obligations, the underlying security instrument grants the lender legal recourse to initiate foreclosure proceedings to recover the outstanding balance.

Core Components of a Mortgage

Every residential mortgage contains several fundamental financial components:

  • Principal: The original sum of money borrowed from the financial institution, which decreases over time as principal payments are applied.
  • Interest: The fee assessed by the lender for the use of the borrowed funds, calculated as a contractual percentage of the remaining principal balance.
  • Term: The contractual life of the loan, specifying the number of years or months over which regular payments are amortized. Common residential terms span multiple decades.
  • Amortization: The structured schedule of periodic payments designed to pay down both interest and principal over the loan term, shifting from interest-heavy payments in early periods toward principal reduction in later periods.
  • Escrow: An account maintained by the loan servicer to accumulate monthly homeowner reserves for property taxes, homeowners insurance premiums, and flood insurance where required.

Fixed-Rate vs. Adjustable-Rate Structures

Lenders offer two primary interest rate structures for first-lien home loans:

  • Fixed-Rate Mortgages: The interest rate remains constant throughout the entire amortization period. Monthly payments of principal and interest do not fluctuate, providing predictable payment schedules.
  • Adjustable-Rate Mortgages (ARMs): The interest rate remains fixed for an introductory period, after which it adjusts periodically based on a published financial benchmark index plus an agreed lender margin, subject to contractual rate adjustment caps.
