Home Equity Loans vs. HELOCs: Closed-End Loans vs. Open-End Lines

Understanding Home Equity Financing

Home equity is the difference between the current value of the home and the amount owed on the mortgage. A home equity loan or a home equity line of credit (HELOC) borrows against that equity and uses the home as collateral. If the outstanding balance is not repaid, the lender can take the home.

If the owner already has a mortgage, a new home equity loan or HELOC is a second mortgage, paid in addition to the first mortgage.

Home Equity Loans

A home equity loan, sometimes called a second mortgage, is a loan for a specific amount secured by the home:

  • Lump-Sum Disbursement: The money is usually paid as a lump sum, rather than as a credit line that can be drawn again.
  • Interest Rate: Home equity loans typically have a fixed annual percentage rate. A home equity loan can also have an adjustable rate.
  • Repayment: The loan is typically repaid with equal monthly payments over a fixed term.

Home Equity Lines of Credit

A HELOC is a revolving line of credit secured by the home, similar to a credit card:

  • Draws: The borrower can draw more than once, up to an approved limit. As the line is paid down, available credit is replenished. During a draw period, the borrower may have to pay only the interest on the amount borrowed.
  • Repayment Period: After the draw period, further borrowing stops and the outstanding balance must be repaid.
  • Variable Rate: HELOCs usually have an adjustable rate, and the payment changes with the outstanding balance. The APR on a HELOC is based on interest and does not include costs such as points.

Structural Comparison

A home equity loan is a set amount, often at a fixed rate. A HELOC is a replenishing line, usually at an adjustable rate. A HELOC rate that can change is not the same kind of rate as a fixed rate on a home equity loan.
