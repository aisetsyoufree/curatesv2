Home Equity Loans vs. HELOCs: Closed-End Loans vs. Open-End Lines

Understanding Home Equity Financing

Home equity represents the unencumbered economic value of a residential property—calculated as the estimated fair market value minus all recorded mortgage liens and encumbrances. When property owners seek to borrow against accumulated equity, lenders offer two primary product structures: closed-end home equity loans and open-end lines of credit.

Both products typically occupy a junior or second-lien position behind the primary first mortgage, which impacts lender risk, underwriting standards, and interest rate margins.

Fixed Home Equity Loans (Closed-End Credit)

A closed-end home equity loan operates similarly to a traditional mortgage installment loan:

  • Lump-Sum Disbursement: The total approved principal balance is funded in a single transaction at loan closing.
  • Fixed Interest Rate: The interest rate remains set for the life of the loan and does not fluctuate with financial market benchmarks.
  • Predictable Amortization: Monthly payments of principal and interest remain identical over a defined term (such as a multi-year repayment schedule).
  • No Reborrowing: As the principal is repaid over time, the available credit does not replenish; the balance steadily amortizes down to zero.

Home Equity Lines of Credit (Open-End Credit)

A Home Equity Line of Credit (HELOC) operates as an open-end revolving credit line secured by the property:

  • Draw and Repayment Periods: A HELOC has two distinct lifecycle stages. During the initial draw period, the borrower may repeatedly borrow, repay, and reborrow funds up to an established credit limit, often making interest-only payments. At the conclusion of the draw period, the line enters the repayment period, during which further draws are prohibited and the remaining balance must be amortized.
  • Variable Benchmark Rates: HELOC interest rates are generally variable and linked to a reference benchmark (such as the Prime rate) plus a stated lender margin. As the underlying benchmark shifts, the borrowing rate adjusts accordingly.
  • Introductory Teaser Rates: Lenders frequently advertise discounted promotional rates that expire after a specified number of introductory months. The subsequent ongoing rate is governed by benchmark fluctuations, subject to contractually established floors and lifetime ceilings.

Structural Comparison

Comparing fixed home equity loans directly with HELOCs requires recognizing their distinct contractual mechanics. A headline introductory rate on a HELOC cannot be equated with a multi-year fixed interest rate on a closed-end loan, because the HELOC rate carries interest rate adjustment risk once the promotional period concludes.
